# Privacy Notice v1.1 and Terms v2.1: what changed and why

For the team's legal person. Written on 20 November 2026 from the code and
the database rules on `main` that day.

## Decisions taken (20 November 2026)

The legal person reviewed this document and approved:

- **every text change below, English and Italian, as drafted;**
- **D1 to D10 as proposed** (for each, the option matching the app), with
  Cloudflare Web Analytics kept on and described (D9);
- **D7: fresh acceptance.** Everyone, existing accounts included, accepts
  Terms v2.1 once on their next visit: `TERMS_VERSION` and
  `TERMS_MIN_VERSION` are both `2.1` (`src/lib/termsVersion.js`);
- **D8:** both texts are dated 20 November 2026, the day they went live.

App changes:

- **A1 built:** the version recorded (`TERMS_VERSION`) and the oldest
  acceptance that still counts (`TERMS_MIN_VERSION`) are separate, so a
  future update that needs no fresh acceptance is recorded with its own
  number without asking everyone again. A unit test fails if
  `TERMS_VERSION` isn't the newest Terms file. Code only; no text describes
  it (the Terms already say a version and its acceptance are recorded).
- **A2, A3 and A4 not built.** Each would contradict a decision just
  approved: A2 (keeping an acceptance record 36 months after deletion)
  contradicts D2 ("deleted with the account"), A3 ("business or private
  person?") is the alternative to the approved D4 option (a), and A4 (a
  second checkbox) needs a list of clauses, which D1 as approved doesn't
  give. Building them would change approved wording without a new review.
  They stay proposals.
- **A5 not built:** D6 proposed no periods, so moderation decisions and
  feedback still had none. (Settled the same day by version 1.2, below:
  D6 decided, A5 built.)

No database migration was needed for v1.1 and v2.1.

- The drafts: `docs/legal/published/privacy-notice-v1.1.html` and
  `docs/legal/published/terms-and-conditions-v2.1.html` (v1.0 and v2.0 are
  kept next to them, unchanged). The Italian courtesy translations are
  `src/legal/privacy.it.json` and `src/legal/terms.it.json`; the side-by-side
  file for review is `docs/i18n/review-legal-it.csv`.
- The pull request's preview shows them on `/privacy` and `/terms` exactly as
  they would go live (switch to IT for the Italian).
- What was **not** changed: the structure, the section numbering, every
  heading anchor (`/terms#section-21` and the others still point to the same
  clauses) and the style.
- Every change below says which text was there, what replaces it, and which
  app behaviour it reflects. The Italian translation follows the English
  change for change, in the same formal style ("l'Utente", "il Gestore");
  the Italian button names are the ones the site shows ("Candidati",
  "Segnala questa offerta", "Elimina il mio account", "Scarica i miei dati").
- At the end: the decisions (D1 to D10, each with what was decided), the
  app changes proposed instead of text changes (A1 to A5, with what was
  built), and things to check that aren't in the code.

## Privacy Notice: every change

### P1. Heading

**Old:**

> **Version 1.0 | Last updated: 11 November 2026**

**New:**

> **Version 1.1 | Last updated: 20 November 2026**

**Why:** New version number. The date is a placeholder: set it to the day of publication (decision D8).

### P2. Privacy at a glance, paragraph 1

**Old:**

> … GigoMe enables students to discover work opportunities and reveal contact information or application links supplied by advertisers. …

**New:**

> … GigoMe enables students to discover work opportunities and obtain the application links or email addresses supplied by advertisers. …

**Why:** There is no "reveal contact" step. A listing has an application link and/or an application email address (no phone field): `job_contacts` has only `apply_url` and `apply_email`.

### P3. Privacy at a glance, paragraph 2

**Old:**

> When a registered student selects **Reveal Contact**, GigoMe records which student account revealed which listing's contact information and the time of the action. **This log is not provided to advertisers and can be viewed only by authorized GigoMe administrators.** It is used for the limited purposes described below.

**New:**

> When a registered student presses **Apply** on a listing, GigoMe shows and opens that listing's application link or email address and records which student account pressed Apply on which listing and the time of the action (an **apply click**). **This record is never shown to advertisers: an advertiser sees only how many times students pressed Apply on each of its listings, never who. Each student can see their own apply clicks; otherwise only authorized GigoMe administrators can view them.** It is used for the limited purposes described below.

**Why:** The button is "Apply" (`jobPage.apply`), and pressing it also opens the link or the email app (`JobDetail.jsx`). Who sees the record was wrong: each student sees their own ("Jobs you opened", `my_opened_jobs()`, and "Download my data"); advertisers see a count per listing on their dashboard (`my_job_stats()`), never who.

### P4. Privacy at a glance, paragraph 3

**Old:**

> … GigoMe uses Supabase for its database and authentication, Cloudflare for hosting and domain-related services, Brevo for authentication emails, and an email inbox service …

**New:**

> … GigoMe uses Supabase for its database and authentication, Cloudflare for hosting, domain-related services, bot protection on the login forms and cookieless visit statistics, Brevo for login-code emails, OpenFreeMap and OpenStreetMap for maps and address search, and an email inbox service …

**Why:** Outside services the visitor's browser talks to that weren't named: Cloudflare Turnstile and Cloudflare Web Analytics (allowed in the Content-Security-Policy in `public/_headers`; Web Analytics is switched on in Cloudflare), OpenFreeMap tiles and OpenStreetMap Nominatim (`src/lib/map.js`, `src/lib/geocode.js`). Brevo sends only login codes (no other email is sent by the app).

### P5. 1. Who controls your personal data?

**Old:**

> The data controller for GigoMe's own processing activities is:

**New:**

> The data controller for GigoMe's own processing activities (referred to in this Notice as the **Operator**, or as "we" or "us") is:

**Why:** The Notice says "we" throughout and "the Operator" once (section 9) without defining either; the Italian translation calls the controller "il Gestore" throughout without defining it. One definition covers both languages.

### P6. 2. Account and identity information

**Old:**

> **Account and identity information:** name or displayed name, email address, account identifier, role (student, business or private advertiser), university affiliation or verified university email where enabled, and account creation/status information.

**New:**

> **Account and identity information:** name, email address, account identifier, role (student or advertiser; GigoMe does not record whether an advertiser is a business or a private individual), the login used to sign up (student or employer), for students the university and how student status was established (an email address of a listed university, or the student's own statement that they are a university student and which university they attend, chosen from a list or typed in), the language used on the site (English or Italian), so that login emails are sent in that language, and account creation, last-login and status information. An account is created when a login code is first requested for an email address, before the code is entered.

**Why:** Only two roles exist (`profiles.account_type`: student, company); nothing records business vs private advertiser. Students can now sign up with any email: `student_verification` is `university_email` or `self_declared`, with `university_id` or the name typed under "Other" (`other_university`). The login used is stored as `signup_as` in the new user's metadata. The site language is stored in the account (user metadata `language`) for the login email's language (`sendCode()`, `EmailLanguageSync.jsx`). The account is created when the code is requested (`signInWithOtp` with `shouldCreateUser: true`), before the code is entered.

### P7. 2. Student profile information

**Old:**

> **Student profile information:** optional educational background, course of study, languages, skills, past experience, general preferences and, if these fields are enabled, age or nationality. These details …

**New:**

> **Student profile information:** name, degree level and expected graduation year. These details …

**Why:** A student profile has exactly three fields the student fills in: `full_name`, `degree_level`, `graduation_year` (`profiles` table, `ProfileFields.jsx`). No course, languages, skills, experience, preferences, age or nationality.

### P8. 2. Advertiser and listing information

**Old:**

> **Advertiser and listing information:** business or individual identity, organization details, professional contact information, listing descriptions, work location, relevant remuneration and contractual information, chosen email address, phone number or external application URL, publication dates and moderation status. Information designated for display on a listing may be visible to Platform users or visitors according to the applicable publication settings.

**New:**

> **Advertiser and listing information:** the advertiser's name (business or individual), website, description and contact email address, listing descriptions, work location (an area of Milan and, optionally, an exact point on the map), relevant remuneration and contractual information, the application email address and/or external application URL, publication and expiry dates, moderation status and notes, and the number of apply clicks and of students following the advertiser. Published listings and approved advertisers' names, descriptions and websites are visible to everyone; the application email address or URL is shown only to logged-in students who press Apply, and the advertiser's contact email address only to the advertiser and administrators.

**Why:** What the app stores about an advertiser: company name, website, description, contact email (`companies`); about a listing: area and optional exact pin (`area_id`, `latitude`/`longitude`), an application link and/or email (no phone field), `published_at`/`expires_at`, status and `review_note`; plus apply-click and follower counts. Visibility as built: listings and approved companies' pages are public; the application contact only after Apply (`reveal_job_contact`); the company contact email only to its owner and admins (`get_company_contact_email()`).

### P9. 2. Contact-reveal records, renamed Apply-click records

**Old:**

> **Contact-reveal records:** student account identifier, listing identifier, timestamp and the technical information reasonably necessary to secure or audit the operation. Only authorized administrators can consult these records. We do not automatically notify advertisers of individual reveals.

**New:**

> **Apply-click records:** student account identifier, listing identifier and timestamp. Each student can see their own records ("Jobs you opened" on the Saved jobs page, and in "Download my data"); advertisers see only the number of apply clicks on each of their listings; otherwise only authorized administrators can consult these records. We do not notify advertisers of individual apply clicks.

**Why:** `job_clicks` holds only the job, the student and the time: no IP address or other technical information (that is in Supabase's own logs, covered under "Authentication and security information"). Who can see the records: see "Privacy at a glance, paragraph 2".

### P10. 2. New paragraph: Things students choose to keep

**Old:** (none: new paragraph after "Apply-click records")

**New:**

> **Things students choose to keep:** saved jobs, saved searches (a name, the search filters and when the search was last opened, to show how many listings are new) and companies followed. In the service they are visible only to the student; a company sees only how many students follow it, never who. No email is sent about them.

**Why:** Not mentioned at all: `saved_jobs`, `saved_searches` (name, filters, `last_seen_at`) and `company_follows`. Readable only by their owner through the app; companies see only `my_company_follower_count()`. Nothing is emailed.

**Why:** In-app channels that weren't mentioned: the Feedback button stores `page` and `message` with `user_id` (`feedback` table); "Report this job" stores `reason`, `details` and `reporter_id` (`job_reports`). Only admins read either.

### P28. 6. Four new paragraphs: OpenFreeMap, Nominatim, Google Maps, the browser's location service

**Old:** (none: four new paragraphs after "Google Gmail")

**New:**

> **OpenFreeMap:** map images. When a page shows a map, the visitor's browser downloads the map from OpenFreeMap, which therefore receives the visitor's IP address, browser information and the part of the map viewed. See https://openfreemap.org/.
>
> **OpenStreetMap Nominatim** (OpenStreetMap Foundation): address search on the listing form. Only when an advertiser presses "Find address" is the address typed in the listing's location field sent from their browser to Nominatim, with their IP address and browser information. >
> **Google Maps:** listing pages offer an "Open in Google Maps" link. >

**Why:** All four are reached from the visitor's browser (HANDOVER.md 7b; CSP in `public/_headers`): map tiles on every map; Nominatim only on "Find address" in the job form (one request per press); Google Maps only when "Open in Google Maps" is clicked (`rel="noreferrer"`); "Use my location" keeps the position in the page only (`Jobs.jsx`). The two new links (openfreemap.org and the OSM Foundation's privacy policy) could not be opened from the build machine: check them (decision D10).

## Decisions for the legal person

The drafts picked, for each point, the option that matches the app. Each
point ends with what was decided on 20 November 2026.

**D1. Separate approval of clauses (Terms 2.4, art. 1341 c.c.).**
The app has one checkbox: "I'm 18 or older and I accept the Terms". 2.4
promises that clauses needing specific approval "shall be presented for
legally effective separate approval", but no such step exists. Draft: 2.4
unchanged. If any clause needs art. 1341/1342 approval (candidates: 12
indemnification, 19.2 limits of liability, 21 unilateral changes, 22.4 if
kept), list them and the team adds a second checkbox naming them (proposal
A4). If none does, say so and 2.4 can stay or go.

**Decided:** 2.4 unchanged, one checkbox; no clause was named for separate approval, so A4 wasn't built.

**D2. Proof of Terms acceptance after an account is deleted (Privacy
section 9, last row; Terms 2.2 and 21.2).**
v1.0 promised up to 36 months after closure; the app deletes the acceptance
with the account and keeps nothing. Draft: "For the life of the account;
deleted with the account". The other option is a minimal record that outlives
the account (account ID, version, time, deleted after 36 months), which needs
an app change (proposal A2). Terms 2.2 and 21.2 say "may be retained", so they
fit either way.

**Decided:** deleted with the account, as drafted; A2 not built.

**D3. Which language prevails (new Terms 22.4).**
The site already shows, above the Italian texts, "Traduzione di cortesia. In
caso di differenze, prevale la versione inglese." The Terms said nothing.
Draft: 22.4 says the English version prevails, "without prejudice to
mandatory consumer-protection rules". Options: keep it; Italian prevails;
both equally authentic; or no clause (then the line above the Italian text
should change too). Please also say whether the Italian Privacy Notice can
stay a courtesy translation for GDPR transparency purposes; the draft adds no
language clause to the Notice.

**Decided:** 22.4 as drafted (English prevails, without prejudice to mandatory consumer-protection rules); no language clause in the Privacy Notice.

**D4. Private advertisers and families (Terms 1.2, section D, 13; Privacy
section 2).**
The app has two roles. A private person or family can sign up at /employers
and must enter a "Company name"; nothing records that they are a private
advertiser, and no category is enabled for them specifically (13.1 says they
may publish "only categories expressly enabled by GigoMe"; the categories are
the same for everyone: hospitality, retail, tutoring & education, office &
admin, marketing & social media, events, tech, other). Draft: the Terms are
unchanged; the Privacy Notice says "role (student or advertiser; GigoMe does
not record whether an advertiser is a business or a private individual)".
Options: (a) keep section D as it is (it applies to whoever is in fact a
private advertiser); (b) GigoMe accepts only businesses and professionals:
section D goes and the employer page says so (admins already review every
company before its jobs go live); (c) the app asks "business or private
person?" at sign-up (proposal A3), and 13.1 then names the categories private
advertisers may use.

**Decided:** option (a), section D unchanged; A3 not built.

**D5. The unused "we/us" and "noi e ci" definition (Terms 1.1).**
The English Terms define "we" and "us" but never use them again; the Italian
translation defines "noi" and "ci" and never uses them either (it says "il
Gestore" throughout). Draft: unchanged. Removing both ("we", "us" in English
and "noi", "ci" in Italian, together) is safe. Your call.

**Decided:** unchanged.

**D6. Records with no retention period.**
